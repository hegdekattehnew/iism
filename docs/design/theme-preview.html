Read this before choosing. A light brand cannot do two jobs with one colour. The product uses its brand as the button fill and as the text of every link, and a colour light enough to look light fails as text on white (aqua and sky as fills are about 2:1 against the page, far under the 4.5:1 a link needs). So the three light palettes split the brand in two: a light fill (step 400, with dark text on it) for buttons, the logo and the progress ring, and a deeper step (700) for links, brand-coloured headings and the focus ring; the table shows each pairing. In the dark theme the light fill is the brand and needs no split. That is one new token pair (a solid and its label) and about thirteen places that today read the brand as a fill. The cost is the button's edge: where the fill is under 3:1 against a white page a shape cannot be seen without its label, so those buttons carry a one-pixel edge in the deeper step (drawn here). Aqua is the lightest and the nearest to the teal you did not want; sky is a clear step away from it and closest to today's blue; indigo is the most distinct and the only one that reads as neither green nor blue. The marigold highlight is the colour the product already uses for a warning, so it is decoration only (the band under the hero, the logo dot) and a test will keep it out of every status. The values are starting points: the tokens story finalises them against the product's own tests, and nothing changes in the product until you decide.
